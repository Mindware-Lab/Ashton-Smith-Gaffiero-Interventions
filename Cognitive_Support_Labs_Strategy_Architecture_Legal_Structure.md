# Cognitive Support Labs

## Building a joint applied-cognition company from a health-psychology beachhead

**Prepared for Dr Daniel Gaffiero**  
**Strategic discussion paper — July 2026**

---

# 1. The opportunity

Cognitive Support Labs should not be conceived simply as a company that produces one or two digital health apps.

The stronger opportunity is to build a **research-led intervention and service-intelligence company** that can repeatedly:

```text
identify where support is failing
→ decide which problems are worth solving
→ design and test interventions
→ build the necessary digital products and service tools
→ measure whether they work
→ integrate them into real institutions
→ publish, license and scale the strongest solutions
```

The initial application would be health psychology, where Dan brings the strongest domain expertise, institutional integration and publication track record.

The deeper capability platform, however, could later be applied to other institutional systems, including corporate resilience, cognitive performance, education and insurance. The company would therefore begin in health psychology without being permanently limited to one condition, one product or one sector.

The strategic principle is:

> **Build one shared capability platform, prove it through a focused health-psychology application, and then extend it into additional sectors with appropriate domain leadership.**

# 2. Why the current business needs to pivot

Mark has developed substantial intellectual property, software, cognitive protocols, apps and public-facing content through HRP Lab Ltd and IQ Mindware.

However, the current direct-to-consumer model has not developed into a viable business. Consumer sales can still provide some income and useful feedback, but they should no longer be treated as the main growth strategy.

The problem is not simply that the underlying ideas lack value. The current route to market is weak:

- intense competition for consumer attention;
- expensive and unreliable app acquisition;
- dependence on continual launches and content production;
- low customer lifetime value;
- limited external validation;
- difficulty converting scientific sophistication into a simple consumer purchase.

The pivot should therefore be:

```text
FROM

consumer apps as the main business
supported by blogging and social media

TO

an institutional research-to-intervention company
supported by partnerships, studies, commissioned work,
custom product development and licensing
```

Selected IQ Mindware products may continue on a small scale, but primarily as a source of:

- limited cash flow;
- product feedback;
- reusable software;
- prototype testing;
- direct contact with users.

They should not determine the architecture of the future company.

# 3. The company thesis

Cognitive Support Labs would help institutions understand and improve psychological and functional support.

Its core proposition could be:

> **We identify where support is inadequate, determine which intervention opportunities offer the greatest value, develop and test proportionate solutions, and integrate the strongest interventions into real services.**

This is broader than app development.

Depending on the problem, the first useful output might be:

- a pathway or provision map;
- a service-gap analysis;
- an intervention-opportunity portfolio;
- a measurement system;
- a redesigned support pathway;
- a low-intensity digital intervention;
- a practitioner tool;
- or a combined digital and human service.

The company should therefore begin with the problem and the institutional pathway—not with a preselected app.

# 4. The shared six-capability platform

The joint company would implement the full HRP Lab Enterprise Capability Model.

The capabilities form one connected sequence.

## 4.1 Understand the system

### Service-pathway, system and risk intelligence

The company first asks:

> Where is support absent, delayed, fragmented, inequitable or poorly matched to need?

Examples include:

- inconsistent adult ADHD waiting-list support across regions;
- limited early support for students with mathematics anxiety;
- gaps between pain assessment and psychological self-management;
- occupational-health pathways that identify distress but provide little functional support.

Typical outputs would include pathway maps, provision indices, user-journey analyses, service-gap reports and candidate intervention points.

This capability can itself produce commissioned revenue before an app is built.

## 4.2 Choose the right opportunity

### Intervention discovery and prioritisation

Not every service gap should become a digital product.

Candidate interventions should be assessed against factors such as:

- importance of the unmet need;
- strength of the mechanism and evidence;
- likely functional and service value;
- suitability for digital delivery;
- user and practitioner acceptability;
- implementation and regulatory burden;
- presence of a credible institutional buyer;
- potential for measurement and scale.

The result should be an explicit decision to develop, partner, defer or reject an intervention.

## 4.3 Engineer the intervention

### Transfer-protocol engineering

The HRP Transfer Lab would turn a selected opportunity into a formal, testable intervention.

The process would include:

```text
mechanism model
→ competing protocol designs
→ computational and synthetic-participant testing
→ prototype
→ small human calibration
→ transfer and functional tests
→ delayed re-check
→ protocol revision
```

The distinctive question is not only whether people improve on a trained exercise.

It is:

> **Does the relevant change survive new examples, changed contexts, independent tasks, meaningful functioning and delay?**

## 4.4 Measure change defensibly

### Measurement and psychometric engineering

Each project would distinguish different kinds of evidence:

```text
need
≠ engagement
≠ task learning
≠ mechanism change
≠ transfer
≠ functioning
≠ service value
≠ economic value
```

The company could develop or adapt:

- validated scales;
- cognitive-affective tasks;
- functional measures;
- provision indices;
- adaptive tests;
- service dashboards;
- confidence labels;
- delayed follow-up measures.

Measurement should be designed alongside the intervention rather than added at the end.

## 4.5 Deliver and integrate the solution

### Digital experience, integration, access and adoption

A scientifically credible intervention is not useful if it cannot be adopted.

The company would therefore design the full delivery system, including:

- user-facing apps or web tools;
- onboarding and eligibility;
- practitioner or manager tools;
- accessibility and localisation;
- escalation and safeguarding;
- service workflow;
- aggregate reporting;
- technical integration;
- training and implementation support.

The product is not only the app. It is the app plus the surrounding pathway.

## 4.6 Validate, publish and mobilise the knowledge

### Research, validation, publication and knowledge mobilisation

The company would build an evidence ladder from early feasibility through to implementation and scale.

Possible outputs include:

- pathway reports;
- preregistrations;
- protocol and psychometric papers;
- feasibility and controlled studies;
- implementation studies;
- partner reports;
- health-economic analyses;
- policy and commissioning briefs;
- case studies.

Publication would strengthen the science, institutional credibility and commercial value of the platform.

## 4.7 Governance across all six

Governance, ethics, assurance and IP stewardship would run across the entire system.

This includes:

- research ethics;
- privacy and health-data rights;
- clinical and product safety;
- safeguarding;
- responsible AI;
- accessibility and equity;
- claims boundaries;
- regulatory classification;
- auditability and human override;
- background and foreground IP;
- publication and confidentiality rules.

# 6. Preferred organisational architecture

The most important architectural distinction is between:

- Mark’s pre-existing assets;
- the new joint operating capability;
- sector-specific applications.

## 6.1 HRP Lab Ltd

HRP Lab Ltd would remain owned and controlled by Mark.

Its main role would be to hold:

- Mark’s pre-existing theories and protocols;
- existing IQ Mindware software and products;
- Trident-G and related background IP;
- existing websites, content and databases;
- reusable code and task components.

HRP Lab Ltd would no longer need to be presented as an already mature institutional platform. It would be the **background-IP, prototyping and legacy-product company**.

## 6.2 The joint operating company

Mark and Dan would jointly build the serious institutional business.

This could initially be Cognitive Support Labs Ltd, provided both agree that its future may extend beyond health psychology.

The joint company would own or control:

- new health-specific protocols;
- jointly developed pathway methods;
- intervention-specific software;
- new measures and item banks;
- synthetic participant models developed jointly;
- practitioner and service tools;
- new datasets;
- implementation methods;
- partner contracts and licences.

The company would operate the six-capability system rather than merely licensing finished products from HRP Lab.

## 6.3 Cognitive Support Labs as the first vertical

Health psychology would be the first sector application because it offers:

- Dan’s specialist expertise;
- active academic and institutional connections;
- credible research and publication routes;
- identifiable service gaps;
- grant and partnership opportunities;
- meaningful functional and service outcomes.

Initial demonstrators might include:

1. exam and mathematics anxiety;
2. persistent pain;
3. adult ADHD provision and interim support.

The first priority should be one complete demonstrator, not three simultaneous apps.

## 6.4 Later corporate expansion

After the capability system has been demonstrated in health psychology, it could be applied to corporate cognition, resilience and performance.

The same underlying capabilities would translate as follows:

| Shared capability | Health application | Corporate application |
|---|---|---|
| System intelligence | Care and support pathways | Workflows and decision systems |
| Opportunity selection | Unmet psychological support | Cognitive-load or performance bottlenecks |
| Protocol engineering | Health-psychology intervention | Attention, evidence and decision support |
| Measurement | Symptoms, functioning and service outcomes | Work quality, errors, judgement and resilience |
| Integration | Practitioner and care pathways | Teams, occupational health and work systems |
| Validation | Health and functional studies | Organisational pilots and performance studies |

This later vertical would probably require an additional leader with expertise in occupational or organisational psychology, human factors, workplace research or enterprise implementation.

The corporate branch should therefore grow from the joint platform—not from IQ Mindware as a separate institutional business.

# 8. Indicative legal and IP structure

This section is a strategic outline rather than legal advice. Formal arrangements would require professional legal, tax and institutional review.

## 8.1 Directors and shareholders

Mark and Dan could both be directors of the joint operating company.

Directorship should not automatically determine:

- equal ownership;
- ownership of all future IP;
- control of every decision;
- rights to Mark’s existing assets;
- treatment of university-related work.

A shareholders’ agreement should address:

- equity and vesting;
- founder roles and time commitments;
- reserved decisions;
- day-to-day authority;
- deadlock resolution;
- founder departure;
- new investors and team members;
- dilution;
- sale or closure;
- future sector expansion.

A 50/50 structure is possible, but only with a robust deadlock mechanism and clear continuing-contribution expectations.

## 8.2 Background IP

Pre-existing assets should be documented before joint development begins.

### Mark/HRP Lab background IP may include:

- Trident-G theory and protocols;
- existing apps and code;
- G Track architecture;
- existing task designs;
- generic Transfer Lab concepts;
- current websites, content and databases.

### Dan or institutional background IP may include:

- existing intervention materials;
- measures and stimuli;
- prior datasets;
- health-psychology methods;
- material developed through KCL or another institution.

Each party should confirm what they own and what they are permitted to commercialise.

## 8.3 Licensing into the joint company

HRP Lab could license selected background IP to the joint company.

The licence should specify:

- field of use;
- exclusivity or non-exclusivity;
- countries;
- sublicensing;
- duration and termination;
- licence fees or revenue share;
- treatment of improvements;
- rights following founder departure.

For example, CSL might receive rights to use selected HRP protocols in defined health-psychology fields, while HRP retains ownership and rights in consumer or unrelated applications.

## 8.4 Foreground IP

New IP created through joint work may include:

- health-specific pathway methods;
- condition-specific protocols;
- software modules;
- item and scenario banks;
- synthetic participant models;
- measures and scoring systems;
- dashboards and implementation systems;
- datasets and health-economic models.

The simplest general principle is:

> **Pre-existing IP remains with its original owner; new health-specific assets funded and created through the joint company belong to or are controlled by the joint company.**

However, improvements to the generic platform may require a more nuanced arrangement, such as:

- joint ownership;
- CSL ownership with a licence back to HRP;
- HRP ownership with a broad CSL licence;
- ownership allocated according to the field of use.

These rules should be agreed before substantial joint development.

## 8.5 University and publication issues

Dan’s institutional role is an important advantage but introduces additional boundaries.

Before projects begin, the parties should clarify:

- whether KCL resources are being used;
- whether KCL owns or may claim particular IP;
- ethics and sponsorship responsibilities;
- data-controller roles;
- conflicts of interest;
- rights to publish;
- confidentiality and publication review;
- use of university names and affiliations;
- ownership of student or collaborator contributions.

Publication should remain a central objective, subject to a reasonable review period for confidentiality and IP protection.

The purpose is to harvest and learn from the existing work while shifting serious growth efforts towards institutional partnerships.

# 10. The initial business model

The first sale does not need to be an app licence.

The most realistic commercial sequence is:

```text
commissioned pathway or provision study
→ intervention-opportunity and feasibility work
→ funded protocol and measurement development
→ custom app or service tool
→ pilot and implementation
→ licence, service contract or international partnership
```

Early revenue opportunities could therefore include:

## Pathway and provision intelligence

For example:

- comparing support across universities;
- mapping ADHD waiting-list provision;
- analysing psychological support within pain pathways;
- assessing digital-support readiness in another country.

## Intervention design and feasibility

For example:

- evidence and mechanism reviews;
- theory-of-change development;
- prioritised intervention portfolios;
- pilot and measurement specifications.

## Funded research and development

Through grants, university collaborations, charities, providers or innovation programmes.

## Custom product development

A university, provider, insurer or medical scheme could fund a tailored intervention and implementation package.

## Licensing and institutional access

Once the intervention is sufficiently validated.

This mixed model reduces dependence on consumer sales and avoids relying exclusively on grants.

# 11. Development strategy

The operating principle should be:

# Capability-complete, product-thin

The company should not attempt to build a universal platform before it has a funded or supported institutional problem.

The first project should create a minimum working version of all six capabilities:

```text
one pathway analysis
→ one opportunity-selection process
→ one protocol
→ one measurement system
→ one digital workflow
→ one validation and publication programme
```

Reusable infrastructure should then be extracted from real projects.

## Phase 1: Founder and legal foundation

- agree the joint company thesis;
- document roles and contributions;
- identify background IP;
- agree provisional ownership and licensing principles;
- clarify KCL boundaries;
- prepare heads of terms and a shareholders’ agreement;
- identify the first institutional problem.

## Phase 2: Secure an anchor

Before substantial platform development, secure at least one of:

- a funded project;
- a commissioned pathway study;
- a university pilot partner;
- a credible grant with institutional backing;
- a provider or international co-development partner.

## Phase 3: Build the first complete capability chain

A plausible first demonstrator is exam and mathematics anxiety:

```text
map university provision
→ identify the most valuable support gap
→ develop the intervention and measures
→ build the minimum app and service workflow
→ run a pilot
→ publish and create an institutional case
```

## Phase 4: Consolidate the shared platform

Extract reusable:

- pathway methods;
- software components;
- measurement tools;
- synthetic participant models;
- governance templates;
- implementation methods.

## Phase 5: Expand carefully

Add a second health application or international partner before creating a full corporate vertical.

## Phase 6: Corporate translation

Recruit an appropriate corporate-domain lead and apply Capability 1 to an actual organisational system before choosing the first corporate product.

# Conclusion

The strategic opportunity is not to rescue the existing consumer-app business or to create a small collection of health apps.

It is to use Mark’s accumulated scientific and technical assets, together with Dan’s health-psychology expertise and institutional research capability, to build a new joint institutional enterprise.

The proposed structure is:

```text
HRP Lab Ltd
holds Mark’s pre-existing IP, software and bounded B2C activity

        ↓ licences selected assets into

Cognitive Support Labs Ltd
Mark and Dan jointly build and operate
the six-capability institutional platform

        ↓ first applied through

health psychology

        ↓ later extended through

corporate and other institutional verticals
with additional domain leadership
```

The first objective is to demonstrate one complete, repeatable capability:

```text
one important institutional problem
→ one defensible pathway analysis
→ one prioritised intervention opportunity
→ one scientifically grounded protocol
→ one usable digital or service solution
→ one credible study
→ one institutional implementation case
```

If that chain works, the company will possess something substantially more valuable than another app:

> **A repeatable system for turning psychological and cognitive science into evidence-generating institutional interventions.**
